# Which Millennium Problems for Biology can public data advance?

**Decision, 26 November 2026. Prioritize Improve Rubisco, Cryopreservation, and The quadruplet cell.** All three have measurements on which a laptop analysis can overturn a plausible interpretation. In the Rubisco compilation, restoring assays separated into different rows raises the best observed specificity above a maize turnover floor from 84.8 to 112, still well short of the historical algal value of 238. In cryopreservation, a twofold decrease in bulk warming rate reverses direction after division by nanoparticle concentration. In quadruplet decoding, a reporter matrix with ten correct row maxima contains zero comparisons isolating the fourth base. These are reproducible results about the evidence for the challenges. Each experimental endpoint remains open in the sources reviewed. [Analysis code](analyze.py) · [Numerical results](results.json)

## Ranking

The names and numbers below follow the organizers’ live definitions. I scored **D** for accessible endpoint-relevant data, **T** for a meaningful and falsifiable laptop result, **I** for scientific or translational impact if solved, and **V** for the path from that result to independent experimental validation. Each runs from 1 to 5. A 1 means little direct evidence or a long unbridged path, 3 means an informative component with a substantial transfer problem, and 5 means unusually strong data, tractability, impact or validation, respectively. The judgmental score is **2D + 3T + I + 2V**, out of 40. A very high impact can coexist with poor prospects for useful computational work. The validation score includes the ability to validate an intermediate prediction. Equal weights retain the same leading three, with cryopreservation next to quadruplet decoding. ## 1. Improve Rubisco — an assay-aware audit of the claimed frontier

**Acceptance test.** The same enzyme, natural or engineered, must equal or exceed *Galdieria partita* in CO₂/O₂ specificity and maize in carboxylation turnover, with each comparison paired to its respective control. A regression slope is beside the point if no enzyme reaches both. Historical control values are screening landmarks. The actual pass thresholds come from the paired measurements. [Official definition](https://millenniumproblems.bio/) · [Algal measurement](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.1997.6497) · [Maize kinetics](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.16.01846)

**Existing results a project would duplicate.** Flamholz et al. already re-examined approximately 300 organisms and found that a one-dimensional trade-off poorly describes form I kinetics. Bouvier et al. found that phylogenetic correction attenuated the observed trade-offs, with their strongest residual association involving CO₂ affinity and turnover. Their later angiosperm analysis inferred improvement in both specificity and turnover over evolutionary history. A 2026 Arabidopsis study also demonstrated increases in turnover, photosynthesis and growth in edited plants, an important application-level comparator. In a direct mutant study, a form II enzyme reached a reported 35.84 s⁻¹ with retained or improved CO₂ affinity. Affinity for CO₂ measures a different quantity from specificity against O₂. The broad de Pins et al. survey measured over 250 bacterial and archaeal enzymes and found roughly 30 s⁻¹ as its upper observed rate at 30 °C. Different samples, temperatures and assay conventions preclude treating either maximum as universal. [Flamholz et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biochem.9b00237) · [Bouvier et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msab079) · [Angiosperm analysis, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2321050121) · [Plant study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73783-w) · [Form II mutant, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2025.151940) · [de Pins et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2501433122)

**What the public source rows support.** I used the 634-row source table at a pinned revision, retaining primary, wild-type form I measurements at 25 °C. Its 466 eligible rows comprise 296 distinct species/strain/cultivar labels after trimming whitespace. Only 130 rows contain both traits. Grouping within label and publication, allowing the two assay rows to differ, yields 226 groups with both measurements. The source records one relevant algal specificity, 238 (reported SD 8.8), with turnover 1.6 s⁻¹, and two maize turnovers, 4.05 and 4.1 s⁻¹. The single algal row’s identifier links to a 1996 methods paper although its text describes the 1997 paper. The latter independently reports 238. Following the identifier alone would misattribute the challenge’s most important reference value. [Pinned source table](https://github.com/flamholz/rubisco/blob/e7441ec736b0a862205dcc0df025176f12f8dc7d/data/DatasetS1_SourceRubiscoKinetics.csv) · [1996 method](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.pcp.a028949) · [1997 measurement](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.1997.6497) · [Other maize comparison](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erm283)

| Evidence rule at turnover ≥4.05 s⁻¹ | Largest observed specificity | Label |
|---|---:|---|
| Both values on one source row | 84.8 | *Panicum deustum* |
| Median of each trait within the same label and paper | 112.0 | *Poa palustris*, 4.2 s⁻¹ |
| Same grouping, omit the paper contributing the maximum | 87.3 | Maize cv. Carella, 4.1 s⁻¹ |

Zero of the 226 within-paper pairs meets both historical cutoffs. The change from 84.8 to 112 reflects how the table represents measurements. Orr et al.’s *Poa* entries list specificity at pH 8.2 and turnover at pH 8.0. The remaining gap, 112/238 ≈ 0.47, is descriptive and restricted to this filtered data release. It does not bound unmeasured enzymes, mutants or other forms. Even a same-paper join cannot establish matched enzyme preparation or the required control run. [Orr et al.](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.16.00750) · [Results](results.json)

**Falsification and next validation.** A table ranking candidates by growth, apparent affinity or turnover alone fails as a detector of the joint endpoint. Prywes et al. already mapped 8,760 of 8,835 possible single substitutions in one enzyme with a growth-coupled assay. Interlaboratory variability persists even after correcting buffer and gas conventions, and a 2025 remeasurement corrected a factor-of-two error in earlier rate reporting. A follow-on analysis should predict the two log ratios to the respective controls, preserve missing traits, and test performance with publications and related clades held out. Reject a proposed improvement if its ranking disappears on assay-aware joins or if either matched-control ratio falls below one. Confidence intervals and a preregistered equivalence rule would clarify close calls. [Growth landscape and code](https://github.com/SavageLab/rubiscodms) · [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08455-0) · [Assay variability](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erab356) · [Rate correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44318-025-00419-y) · [Independent survey code](https://gitlab.com/milo-lab-public/rubisco_is_slow)

## 2. Cryopreservation — separating scale, material and survival

**Acceptance test.** An intact, live, wild-type adult mouse must recover after at least 24 hours of whole-body freezing or vitrification, with viability above 99% and no permanent injury. Ethics approval is required. The organizers permit but discourage somatic engineering. The evidence branches are historically distinct. Hamsters survived partial crystallization in early work. Vitrified rat kidneys stored up to 100 days restored life-sustaining renal function after transplant. A 2026 study recovered electrophysiological activity in adult mouse hippocampi after in-situ brain vitrification. A 14 October 2026 **preprint** reports human-scale pig kidneys stored up to one year, perfusion function and immediate urine production following transplant. The latter is already pertinent prior art against claiming a first successful scale-up from rodent kidneys. Its acute endpoints leave long-term graft survival uncertain. [Hamsters](https://doi.org/10.1038/1731136a0) · [Rat transplants](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38824-8) · [Murine hippocampus](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2516848123) · [Pig-kidney preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.756990)

**Reanalysis of Gangwar et al.’s source workbook.** Figure 3d supplies three independent cryoprotectant bags per geometry. The measured mean center cooling rates at 0.5, 1 and 3 L were 1.280, 0.983 and 0.507 °C/min. The authors’ corresponding model values were 1.36, 0.96 and 0.45, with signed relative errors of +6.3%, −2.4% and −11.2%. This is a benchmark on the original data, not independent validation of their model. In this experiment 3 L is the vitrification result; the largest bulk nanowarming demonstration was **2 L**. The authors also report largely vitrified porcine liver with regional ice near the portal vein/fat. A single bulk temperature trace would miss that outcome. [Primary paper and source workbook](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63483-2) · [Authors’ COMSOL models](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16716098)

| Figure 7c, M22 | Iron concentration (mg Fe/mL) | Warming rate, mean ± between-bag SD (°C/min) | Rate divided by concentration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 L, three bags | 10.7 | 173.52 ± 3.14 | 16.22 |
| 2 L, three bags | 4.6 | 87.96 ± 1.51 | 19.12 |

I averaged left, center and right probes within each bag before calculating SD. The three probe readings are repeated spatial measurements. The 2 L/1 L raw rate ratio is 0.507; after dividing by iron concentration it is 1.179. **The sign of the apparent volume effect is unidentifiable here.** The calculation is a sensitivity check under proportional heating. It cannot estimate the causal effects of volume and concentration from two collinear groups. Its result also depends on spatial sampling and material properties. The authors explicitly identify the concentration difference. [Figure 7 source data](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12474906.1/41467_2025_63483_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx)

**Falsification and next validation.** A new scale model should beat the published cooling predictions on withheld geometries and predict worst-region temperature or ice observations, with uncertainty, at matched material conditions. A model trained on homogeneous bags loses its tissue-level claim if independent tissue regions outside its predicted failure zone contain ice or lose function. Public bulk warming data cannot settle where the sign changes in tissue. Even perfect thermal agreement would leave toxicity, perfusion heterogeneity, brain function and delayed injury unmeasured. For orientation only, with independent identically distributed outcomes and zero failures, 299 successes give a one-sided exact 95% lower bound above 0.99. This arithmetic illustrates how little a handful of cases says about the threshold. The organizers prescribe neither that confidence rule nor that sample size. ## 3. The quadruplet cell — what does a diagonal decoding matrix establish?

**Acceptance test.** Every protein-coding sequence in a replicating cell, including its translation machinery, must be read in consecutive nonoverlapping four-base units, with triplet decoding below detection. Mutating each of the four positions must have a similar probability of changing amino-acid identity. The site specifies no tolerance or codon-use weighting for “similar.” Those choices materially change the test. [Official definition](https://millenniumproblems.bio/)

There are 256 four-base words and 384 undirected single-substitution pairs **per position**, or 1,536 total. To illustrate the weakness of table design alone, randomly permute 256 assignments to 20 amino acids with 16 classes of size 13 and four of size 12. Under uniform codons and substitutions the expected change probability is 0.95368 at *every* position by symmetry. This toy calculation excludes termination and says nothing about reading a frame. Calles et al. already published quadruplet fail-safe codes and code for exploring them. DeBenedictis et al. demonstrated multiplex decoding of four distinct quadruplets; their later panel covered all 20 tRNA classes, but only nine of 20 synthetases tolerated quadruplet anticodons by their criteria. A mitochondrial study added quadruplet decoding in yeast against a background of nuclear-encoded machinery. [FSQUAD paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkz745) · [Public code](https://github.com/EndyLab/codon-tables/tree/manuscript) · [Multiplex decoding](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25948-y) · [Tolerance study](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.76941) · [Mitochondrial test](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssynbio.4c00630)

**Empirical test.** In the 2022 Figure 6B source matrix, all 10 reporter maxima occur at the intended codon. The ten reporters also have ten distinct three-base prefixes. The number of single-substitution pairs in the panel is **4, 2, 1, 0** for positions 1 through 4. A lookup using only the first three positions can therefore reproduce all ten preferred codon labels. This is an identifiability result about this panel. The readout is luminescence and the entries include negative background-corrected values, so treating them as probabilities of amino-acid incorporation would be an error. [Figure 6B matrix](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC9094753.1/elife-76941-fig6-data2.csv)

**External falsification check.** The stronger hypothesis that the fourth base has no measurable effect fails against the 2021 paper. I re-read its separate Figure 4a workbook and averaged the available replicates per reporter for each of five tRNAs. Changing UAGA to UAGG retained **4.4–24.4%** of the cognate signal across these five rows. This both detects a fourth-position effect and demonstrates residual cross-decoding. It measures one prefix and one alternative final base in this calculation. It cannot specify the probability of a nonsynonymous mutation across a living proteome. [Figure 4a workbook archive](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC8481270.1/41467_2021_25948_MOESM3_ESM.zip)

A follow-on benchmark should score measured, missing and unresolved codon comparisons separately, report uniform and usage-weighted position effects, and distinguish stop/unassigned outcomes from amino-acid substitutions. The first-three-base lookup is a negative control. Withdraw a code-wide discrimination claim if it performs equally well on position-balanced, held-out comparisons, or if triplet reading remains detectable above a declared assay limit. Replication and translation of the entire machinery remain separate organism-level criteria.

## Five claims a new effort must beat

1. **“Our Rubisco breaks the trade-off.”** Improved affinity, a new subgroup correlation or a faster sequence is already within published precedent. Show the same enzyme meeting both named paired-control thresholds, with uncertainty. For an interim computational result, beat a source- and clade-held-out two-trait baseline. [Comparative analysis](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msab079) · [Mutant result](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2025.151940)
2. **“Nanowarming scales.”** Liter-scale physical warming, life-sustaining rat renal grafts and reported acute human-scale pig-kidney recovery already exist. Add an independent geometry or regional injury prediction at controlled conditions. Organism-level survival requires its own evidence. [Physical scale](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63483-2) · [Rat](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38824-8) · [Pig preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.756990)
3. **“The code uses all four bases.”** Balanced paper tables are easy and published multiplex decoding is real. Beat the three-base-prefix lookup on comparisons differing at position four, check all positions, and quantify residual triplet reading in the relevant biological context. [Design prior art](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkz745) · [Crosstalk assays](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25948-y)
4. **“We can design a protease on demand.”** The comparator already includes 135 designed proteases, selected protein targets and cellular applications. Report one denominator per preregistered site, design time, catalytic efficiency and measured proteome-wide collateral cleavage. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11167-2)
5. **“This is the first unrelated nitrogenase.”** An earlier public design claim exists. Establish the structural and sequence exclusion, isotope attribution and calibrated turnover relative to natural enzymes, and reconcile the result with that disclosure. [ACS meeting record](https://acs.digitellinc.com/p/s/design-of-an-artificial-nitrogenase-that-reduces-dinitrogen-to-ammonia-648930)

## Reproduction, access and scope

Run `python3 analyze.py` from this directory. It reads the included source files offline using the Python standard library and reproduces [results.json](results.json). [Research notes](notes.md) identify source revisions, licenses, access issues and limitations. The [bibliographic ledger](bibliography.json) gives article titles, DOIs, registration-supplied publication dates and first authors. Online-first, issue and preprint dates can differ. Literature searches covered public primary papers, conference/preprint records, public sequence deposits, source-data supplements and authors’ code up to the review date. This was a bounded search, so negative findings refer to the sources located. No human or animal experiments were performed. No research refusals were observed.
