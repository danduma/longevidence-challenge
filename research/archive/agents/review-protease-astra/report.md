# What survives the ProtRec holdout audit?

Independent computational review, 26 November 2026. **The additive model’s error increase on unseen substrate combinations reproduces. Its below-prevalence average precision depends on which control measurements define a positive.** Three stop-containing reporter controls occupy the top three places in the scout’s reference distribution, although the modeled substrates all lack stops. Matching control eligibility to the prediction task reverses the sign of the AP comparison without changing a single prediction.

The bounded additional result concerns **recurrence of reporter background**. On 139 shared substrates, high background in two negative constructs identifies 6–8 of the 14 highest-background substrates in the third. This association survives both prespecified panel restrictions. Evidence for transport to a fourth construct is weak, and an earlier screen has too few complete measurements to validate transport at the fixed depth. The evidence supports a within-screen assay finding. It establishes neither a transferable correction nor prospective protease performance.

## Independent reproduction

I checked the four input files against the scout inventory’s SHA-256 hashes and implemented the analysis from the tabular data. Raw DMS input contains 413,779 distinct enzyme-label × substrate pairs. Filtering sequentially leaves 316,040 at minimum depth 100, 297,957 with reported enzyme mutation count ≤1, 295,822 with a seven-residue standard-amino-acid substrate, and 294,557 with a parent or single-substitution enzyme label. This last step excludes stop changes including stop-loss labels. There are 4,411 enzyme labels and 161 motifs. “wt” denotes the scan parent, the engineered TEVp 0. Recomputed substrate distances from the canonical peptide agree with every retained annotation. The original experiment targeted the parent and single substitutions on both sides. The multi-mutant substrate tail is outside that intended substrate panel. [Huber et al., Fig. 2 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-60622-7)

The target is the dimensionless time-average of the flipped-DNA fraction across the four-hour interval. Trapezoidal integration of five F/(F+N) measurements reproduces every distributed DMS and control AUC within 0.00010. The stored minimum equals the smallest F+N over the five times for every row. Reading depth concerns counting precision. It cannot establish cleavage or remove barcode/backbone artifacts. The authors explicitly checked mutated backbones and suspiciously discordant barcodes before their final DMS analysis. The scout and this reproduction use the distributed mapped input, upstream of that correction. Both splits use the original row order and seed. I solved the ridge normal equations directly, with a unit penalty on every coefficient, including the constant column added after target centering. The scout’s comment about an unpenalized intercept is imprecise. The training mean is a fixed offset, and the fitted constant adjustment is penalized. Position-specific nonreference residues and enzyme identities enter additively. There are no interaction terms. Average precision (AP) uses tied-score groups. Constant prediction gives the prevalence exactly. The substrate-mean lookup falls back to the training mean for every blocked row. Random test rows share both enzyme and motif identities with training, with zero duplicate pairs. The blocked test shares all 3,701 tested enzyme identities with training and holds out every occurrence of 25 double, one triple and one six-substitution motif. Every nonzero design column in test occurs in training. This rules out unsupported individual indicators as the explanation. It omits training-set, shared-enzyme, biological-repeat and assay uncertainty. These 27 motifs do not constitute a probability sample of new substrates. ## Why the AP conclusion changes

The scout pools the three negative constructs used in the authors’ noise analysis. These are binding-negative controls. The file also contains MBP, catalytically inactive full-length enzymes and a degradation-tag control with different interpretations. Of the 467 pooled rows, 23 have depth below 100 and three carry the same stop-containing substrate. The latter three have AUC .2402–.2715. They account for three of five controls above the original 99th percentile. Truncation at this site changes a reporter whose output depends on a downstream degradation tag. High flipping in these controls is a poor reference for the high tail of a population restricted to intact peptides. Huber’s final DMS control script explicitly excluded this stop and restricted its substrate noise panel to parent and singles. Their noise blanking used a pooled fitted-normal threshold and mean. The scout’s empirical 99th percentile and motif-wise subtraction are separate choices. ## How much background transfers?

For each held-out control construct, I averaged the other two AUCs per substrate and flagged the top decile. The held-out construct supplied all outcomes for that rotation. All three measurements had to meet the depth and substrate criteria. This gives equal weight to each shared motif and tests agreement independently of the six active-enzyme biochemical panels.

| Held-out construct | Shared motifs | Spearman ρ | Held-out top-decile motifs among 14 flagged | Held-out base rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (1–13)-mCherry | 139 | .398 | 7/14 | 14/139 |
| I C151A 30*31* | 139 | .417 | 8/14 | 14/139 |
| No TEVp | 139 | .453 | 6/14 | 14/139 |

All three meet the predeclared direction and enrichment criteria. With only parent and single mutants, the overlaps are 7, 9 and 6 out of 14 on 132 motifs. Without a depth restriction, they are 8, 7 and 6 out of 15 on 150 motifs. The rotations reuse the same motifs and constructs. Their agreement cannot distinguish a sequence effect on reporter stability from shared barcode, library, or other technical effects. [Control transfer results](results/control_transfer.csv)

A supplementary fourth-construct test weakens the case for broad transfer. With all four constructs at depth ≥100, only 59 motifs remain. The original three controls’ mean has ρ=.031 with MBP, with two of six top-decile calls overlapping against a base rate of 6/59. The authors also flagged MBP underrepresentation in their between-experiment comparison. For a genuinely different screen, I applied their screen 3 library and duplicate rules and required complete depth-qualified data for all three original constructs. Only three motifs qualify in replicate 1 and one in replicate 2. Both fall below the prespecified ten-motif minimum. There is **no cross-screen validation** at this depth. ## What the 802 biochemical matches establish

The six selected enzymes each have 7 × 20 entries in the relative initial-rate file. Mapping position and amino acid to the full substrate collapses seven canonical representations to one per enzyme. All canonical relative rates equal 1. This accounts for 840 entries becoming 804 distinct biochemical pairs. Two lack matched reporter data, parent 0 × ENLVFQS and NX2 × ANLYFQS. Each of the 802 remaining reporter keys is unique. Eleven matched reporter measurements have depth below 100. The scout applied the depth cutoff to its model, not this join. Negative relative slope estimates were retained. The relative file is dimensionless, normalized to the canonical substrate, and supports within-enzyme comparisons. The source files provide no physical unit for the absolute-rate column in their header. I used that column only to check ranks. [Pestalozzi’s original work](https://doi.org/10.3929/ethz-b-000370837) · [ProtRec biochemical data](https://github.com/JeschekLab/ProtRec/tree/8b9a1865108fa44c3e0a24ac20a59754222847ca/2%20Data%20analysis%20(Lukas%20Huber)/In%20vitro%20data%20by%20Luzius%20Pestalozzi)

Within-enzyme Spearman correlations of raw AUC with relative initial rate range from .620 to .816. Subtracting the same motif’s mean control AUC improves four and worsens two. NX1 drops from .715 to .700, and NX2 from .816 to .808. Absolute-rate ranks yield raw correlations .633–.819. These are orthogonal assays on selected variants and related single-mutant substrates. They offer no biochemical measurements for any of the 27 blocked motifs. The 802 matches should not be counted as independent experiments or prospective successes. The authors’ final comparison also replaced reporter measurements of the manually cloned parent with the deeper DMS equivalent, then duplicated the canonical reporter entry across the seven position slices for comparison. Their script computes correlations with both relative and absolute rates. The broader fact of assay agreement is established prior work. ## Prior art and novelty verdict

MLDEEP addresses combinatorial extrapolation principally on the **enzyme** axis. Its preprocessing combines two screens, takes replicate 1 of the first, excludes stop-containing protease sequences, computes screen-specific control p-values and pivots the data to 20 P1′ outputs per enzyme. Its split code withholds complete enzyme profiles within designated partitions and checks enzyme-sequence separation. The published MLDEEP job uses 1,000 AB test enzymes or 500 AC, training depth 100 and a 6,500-measurement training cap. The MLP uses 20 logits with read-weighted focal loss. The paper reports hysteresis thresholds .01/.1; the archived data-module default and data configuration specify .01/.05. Verifying exact resolved configurations would matter for a reproduction. This audit makes no MLDEEP performance comparison. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-60622-7) · [Preprocessing and data module](https://github.com/BorgwardtLab/MLDEEP/tree/8cd6c319433be71644e79249a442b659d607c441) · Experiment command (archival input; see source-downloads.json and the source URLs) · MLP source (archival input; see source-downloads.json and the source URLs)

Searches on 26 November 2026 combined ProtRec/MLDEEP and TEV with substrate holdout, combinatorial substrate extrapolation, negative-control deciles, MBP, stop-codon control thresholds, average precision, reporter artifacts and Pestalozzi initial rates. I checked primary papers, preprints, author documentation, and the release trees and analysis scripts. Structure-aware TEV work already evaluated predictive models on combinatorial substrates, and Bioverse documents a compatibility benchmark using Huber data. Huber’s paper and code already establish binding-related recorder signal, control blanking, barcode artifacts and reporter–FRET agreement. The authors’ internal-control code even compares controls between screens, using a different aggregation. [Structure-aware TEV study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10523478/) · [Earlier preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949123/) · [Bioverse benchmark documentation](https://borgwardtlab.github.io/bioverse/implementations/benchmarks.html) · [ProtRec authors’ code](https://github.com/JeschekLab/ProtRec)

**What appears unreported in the sources checked** is the exact leave-one-negative-construct-out decile recurrence on this panel and the reversal of the scout’s AP-versus-prevalence comparison under eligibility-matched controls. Both are reproducible properties of specified public data. No general biological novelty or improvement in protease engineering follows. The strongest next public-data experiment is to reconstruct the authors’ barcode/backbone-corrected DMS and control tables from the upstream barcode-level files, then repeat this locked threshold comparison and control-transfer test on the common keys. This would directly distinguish sensitivity to preprocessing from persistence in the release’s final analysis population. [Upstream data and correction scripts](https://github.com/JeschekLab/ProtRec/tree/8b9a1865108fa44c3e0a24ac20a59754222847ca)

Problem 08 requires prospective performance on 20 blinded, preregistered accessible sites in folded endogenous proteins, with greater than 80% success, a 24-hour design window, cellular cleavage and comparative catalytic efficiency and proteome-wide off-target performance. A bacterial DNA recorder and a FRET linker assay measure local peptide preference in engineered contexts. Neither measures access to those endogenous sites or global off-target cleavage. Even the challenge’s peptide-only partial milestone requires prospective designs for preregistered sequences. This retrospective audit provides no success-rate estimate for either milestone. [Official challenge specification](https://millenniumproblems.bio/)
