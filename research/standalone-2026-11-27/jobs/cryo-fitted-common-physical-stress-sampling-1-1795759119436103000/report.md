# Common physical points expose a corner-sensitive stress ranking

The paired reversal is sensitive to how the shared points are placed. With two Gauss nodes per fine-cell axis, the sampled tensile peak still reverses across meshes, while the volume-weighted smooth score favors graded on both. With three nodes per axis, both scores favor uniform strict on both meshes. The three-node peak locations lie near degenerate corners of the cube map, where stresses are far larger than the native cell-center peaks. This makes the fitted runner-up order unsuitable as a mesh-independent benchmark and also qualifies the prior smooth and hard lead of the tuned uniform comparator.

## Controlled comparison

We reran the twelve fitted configurations from the [predecessor report](sources/fitted-report.md), retaining its three schedules, 0.75 mm cut-cell thermal histories, nested 192- and 1,536-element mechanical meshes, two phase-overlap integration orders, stress-free start, and mechanical time samples. Each trajectory has a common 0.30 s cadence and a final schedule-specific sample at the −35 °C coldest-heart event. We repeated the scoring over the common first 12 s. The native comparator uses each mesh's own cell centers and the inherited unnormalized log-sum-exp with a 20 kPa scale. Its sampled hard peak is the largest major principal stress over those centers and times. [Pinned Vitrify mechanics solver](https://github.com/Marc-Dvci/Vitrify/blob/1d498ee5c583e0112a762e60892d576ec2e27f57/tesseracts/mechanics/solver.py), [release coupling code](https://github.com/Marc-Dvci/Vitrify/blob/1d498ee5c583e0112a762e60892d576ec2e27f57/coupler/workflow.py).

Both clouds use tensor Gauss nodes on the finer reference partition, which is a common refinement of both partitions. Applying the common map gives the same physical points and physical volume weights in both solves. The rules have 12,288 and 41,472 points, all strictly within both owning elements. Their volumes are 2,199.760 and 2,200.069 mm³, against the analytic 2,200.108 mm³. At each point we differentiate the owning element's Q1 displacement through the map Jacobian and use that element's piecewise constant Maxwell history, effective shear and thermal eigenstrain. We form the full stress tensor there and evaluate its three eigenvalues. This evaluation leaves the native stiffness assembly and center history updates intact. [Nowell's cube-to-sphere map](https://mathproofs.blogspot.com/2005/07/mapping-cube-to-sphere.html), [FEniCS DG0 discussion](https://fenicsproject.discourse.group/t/projection-of-dg0-function-over-non-matching-meshes/13810).

For the shared smooth score, the exponential of each principal stress divided by 20 kPa is weighted by the point's quadrature volume divided by the total cloud volume. We sum those terms across points, eigenvalues and inherited time samples, then take the logarithm and multiply by 20 kPa. Each time sample receives the same temporal weight as in the predecessor. Absolute smooth values have a different normalization from the native objective. The table compares within-mesh schedule gaps, for which a common normalization offset cancels.

The tuned uniform release-policy schedule retains the lowest native scores and the lowest two-node hard peaks. On the fine mesh its two-node smooth score is 0.099–0.112 kPa above graded. Its three-node smooth and hard scores exceed uniform strict on both meshes. Schedule-specific absolute scores, peak locations and the full scored time traces are in [results.json](results.json).

The three-node rule samples closer to reference corners. Every three-node peak across the twelve solves occurs at 7.5 s near a cube corner, where the physical map Jacobian has condition number about 462. The analytic Jacobian has rank two at the exact corners. The physical strain uses its inverse, which can amplify reference-space displacement gradients near those corners. Those locations produce sampled major principal stresses of 2.892–6.139 MPa, compared with 1.120–1.180 MPa at the native centers. A single peak term contributes 14–38% of the whole-history volume-weighted smooth sum despite its small quadrature weight. Every two-node peak occurs at a point with Jacobian condition number below 3. Accurate integration of total volume offers little assurance for this exponentially weighted stress score. The localization implicates the mapped kinematics and warrants separating map conditioning from the inherited nodal constraints and temperature representation.

## Scope and precedent

The predecessor established the native reversal under a fixed fitted boundary. Vitrify's upstream grid sweep changes thermal and mechanical meshes together. Joshi and Rabin use finite-element major principal stresses at representative locations and domain maxima, with cooling history carried into rewarming. Farrell and Maddison provide a prior common-refinement integration method. FEniCS Discourse describes cell ownership in DG0 point evaluation. CryoNet and Biostasis discuss uneven warming and glass-transition stress. The public Vitrify issue and pull request snapshots were empty at retrieval. The increment here is a controlled spatial rescore of the existing paired fitted solves and a localization of the resulting ranking sensitivity. [Vitrify grid study](https://github.com/Marc-Dvci/Vitrify/blob/1d498ee5c583e0112a762e60892d576ec2e27f57/experiments/grid_convergence.py), [Joshi and Rabin](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0290063), [Farrell and Maddison](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2010.07.015), [CryoNet](https://www.cryonet.org/cgi-bin/dsp.cgi?msg=18925), [Biostasis](https://www.biostasis.com/new-warming-breakthrough-for-cryopreserved-organs/).

The calculation compares two discrete solutions under two finite rules. The shared partition is aligned with the fine mesh to avoid straddling either mesh's DG0 jumps. The cloud change alters both point placement and weights. A converged volume objective, a continuum tensile maximum, and biological fracture risk remain unresolved. The tuned uniform schedule can still serve as the historical comparator. Use a map with controlled element Jacobians before attributing the shared-point stress ranking to the temperature fields or promoting any schedule on that ranking.

## Reproduce

Run `sh verify.sh` from this directory. The shell uses Python with NumPy and SciPy or creates a local environment with the pinned requirements. The verifier regenerates the thermal histories and reruns all twelve solves. It independently constructs the two clouds, uses an analytic geometry Jacobian and differentiates the Q1 basis in reference coordinates, then compares scores to the two local archives and evaluates the ranking and corner diagnostics. `python3 -B analysis.py` with the dependencies available regenerates the result archive.
