# How much does the radial loading profile contribute to Vitrify’s stress reduction?

The independent calculation recovers Vitrify’s original stress comparison. The released design already recommends near-saturation loading and an early RF throttle. The question here is whether the detailed radial profile earns its complexity once total surrounding iron and the duty schedule are held fixed. Extending the run to the first time the coldest heart voxel reaches −35 °C leaves the tensile peaks early in the run. The main new comparison holds the optimized RF schedule, surrounding iron mass, and heart loading fixed. At 1.4 mm, replacing the three-band loading profile with a uniform surrounding solution changes the tensile peak by 0.00093 MPa. In a separate control with the optimized loading field fixed, replacing the RF schedule with full power changes the peak by 0.10892 MPa. The simpler uniform-loading, staged-power protocol is therefore a useful comparator for any claim that precise radial grading contributes material benefit in this phantom.

The unnormalized smooth objective depends on the number and weights of sampled stresses, so relative comparisons here use a common cadence and temperature event.

At 1.2 mm the uniform-minus-graded difference is 0.00058–0.00059 MPa in peak tension and 0.00062 MPa in the smooth objective at mechanics intervals of 0.350 and 0.140 s. At the finer of those cadences, the respective peak tensions are 1.19723 and 1.19664 MPa. Both schedules pass the rate and terminal screens at 1.2 mm, and the uniform surround reaches the threshold 0.048 s sooner. The absolute peak shift when refining the spatial grid is over an order of magnitude larger than either same-grid grading contrast. The two grids support keeping early duty control in the comparison and carrying forward a uniform-load comparator before attributing material benefit to the three-band profile. They offer limited spatial convergence evidence. Upstream itself reports a rate-screen failure at a different resolution (1.1 mm) for the unmodified zero-margin design.

## Check against the released rate-margin design

The upstream project already reoptimized with a 1.5 °C/min design-grid buffer to the all-time specimen-wide rate floor. At its documented 1.1 mm validation grid, both the released graded design and the matched-iron uniform surround retain a modeled whole-specimen rate above 51 °C/min through the coldest-heart cutoff, using the same RF schedule and 9.29409 mg Fe/mL mean surrounding loading. The graded rate of 51.27326 °C/min matches the published fixed-horizon grid check. The uniform surround reaches the heart cutoff 0.0624 s sooner and raises peak tension by 0.001695 MPa and the smooth objective by 0.001485 MPa at the common 0.235 s mechanics cadence. Both peak at 8.461 s. This single-grid check extends the ablation to a design with a positive rate margin on the mesh where the zero-margin design falls below 50 °C/min. Robustness on additional meshes and under other SAR laws remains open. ## Method and controls

- The geometry is Vitrify’s analytic voxelized rat-heart bag. We independently reconstructed the six-parameter loading map and checked the 1.4 mm arrays against the released figure cache. The independent thermal integrator uses the source’s harmonic face conductivity, explicit Euler step, piecewise temperature-dependent material properties, adiabatic boundary, and extrapolated low-temperature SAR. The 70 s duty schedule retains its original physical-time knots on the upstream discrete thermal grid. If arrest occurs after that integration horizon, duty stays at its last value. Changing the nominal upstream duration would rescale the schedule, confounding the endpoint comparison.
- We find the first crossing of the coldest heart voxel using the myocardium and chamber labels, and also report the event for the entire specimen including the surrounding solution. We interpolate the final within-step crossing cell by cell. Mechanics takes a fractional final step at the event. The rate columns use the source’s explicit left-state derivative. Pre-crossing rates are tabulated only through the arrest interval.
- The mechanics solver independently assembles a structured trilinear hexahedral equilibrium problem using two-point tensor quadrature, Vitrify’s six pinned displacement components, and its compliant ersatz domain. Cell-center strains feed the source’s exponential deviatoric Maxwell update with viscosity evaluated at the interval midpoint, using a constant bulk modulus and a thermal expansion coefficient of 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹. It starts at uniform −150 °C with zero stress. It computes principal stresses and applies the nested log-sum-exp equivalently as a single reduction with a 20 kPa smoothing scale. Banded Cholesky replaces PETSc, and does not use cached stresses as solver inputs.

## Prior work and interpretation

Joshi and colleagues (2022, rat Case I and Cases II–IV) already specify a coldest-heart cutoff of −35 °C and the 0 °C hot ceiling. Joshi and Rabin (2023, Table 2 and Figs. 3–6) supply the VS55 constitutive laws and document early stress peaks and relaxation as the CPA warms. Their simulation includes a prior cooling phase and residual stress. Biostasis’s specialist discussion already identifies glass-transition warming as critical for stress. CryoNet discusses selective magnetic heating of poorly equilibrated regions as early as 2002. The later popliteal artery study reinforces that stress benefits depend on geometry, boundary conditions, and excitation. Vitrify already released its horizon check and a spatial refinement comparison against the 7.5 mg/mL baseline. The public C01–C05 reassessment already distinguishes warming-rate proxies from stress. The increment here is the matched-iron mechanical ablation through the physical endpoint. [2022 thermal paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8823202/?report=xml), [2023 mechanical paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0290063), [2026 artery paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0350078), [Biostasis](https://www.biostasis.com/new-warming-breakthrough-for-cryopreserved-organs/), [CryoNet](https://www.cryonet.org/cgi-bin/dsp.cgi?msg=18925), [pinned Vitrify](https://github.com/Marc-Dvci/Vitrify/tree/1d498ee5c583e0112a762e60892d576ec2e27f57).

This is a comparison of specified schedules and loading fields in one surrogate geometry and one SAR model. The full-power control identifies a conditional effect of staging at the chosen load. The matched uniform control holds the released RF schedule fixed. Joint optimization within the uniform design class remains open. The stress differences are numerical model outputs. Physical equivalence, fracture rates and preservation outcomes remain untested. Cooling-history residual stresses, measured loading heterogeneity, hardware feasibility, discretization error beyond the resolutions tested, and post-RF equilibration remain outside the calculation.

## Reproduce

With Python 3.14 and uv available, run `sh verify.sh` and `sh verify.sh margin` from this directory. The wrapper installs pinned NumPy and SciPy in a job-local virtual environment and cache. The original coordinator invocation stopped at the NumPy import because it used the system Python. With the pinned packages already installed, run `python -B verify.py` and `python -B margin.py` directly. The verifier regenerates temperature and stress histories from public geometry and parameters, checks the published coarse stress and thermal metrics, checks that the crossing is the first bracketed event, asserts the paired comparison, and checks [results.json](results.json) against the fresh calculation. The calculations run offline and can take several minutes. First-time dependency setup uses the package index. The cached Vitrify repository preserves its Apache-2.0 license. The two PLOS XML files preserve their CC BY 4.0 notices. The snapshots of the 2022 ASME paper and the two specialist discussions contain short excerpts with attribution.
