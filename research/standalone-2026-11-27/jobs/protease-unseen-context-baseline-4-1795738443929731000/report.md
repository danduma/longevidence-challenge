# The held-position reporter gain depends on which rows fit the calibration

On 780 previously matched pairs, both reporter calibrations had higher mean position RMSE than a sequence-context mean when fitted on all training rows. Both had lower mean RMSE when fitting was restricted to rows whose predictions were left open by the same context rules. Inner position holdouts recovered the restricted fit for every outer fold where the reporter could affect predictions. This is a retrospective benchmark of relative initial-rate prediction on the ProtRec panel.

## What was held out

Each fold withholds all noncanonical substitutions at one position across the same six enzymes. The original common-key and depth criteria are retained. The 170 negative rate estimates remain signed. The verifier reconstructs the 840 deposited signed rate ratios from initial slopes divided by the corresponding enzyme-position canonical slope. Canonical rows serve as controls for defining the target and are excluded from fitting and scoring. Neither model uses held-position biochemical measurements or slopes as predictors. The reporter model assumes corrected test-pair AUC is available. The context model uses the position, substitution and enzyme identity. [C10 protocol and provenance](https://github.com/danduma/longevidence-challenge/tree/main/research/contributions-2026-11-26/c10_calibration) · [ProtRec release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15346003)

## Shared context and training choices

Huber et al. describe P5 tolerance, P1 constraint, and acidic and aliphatic preferences at P6 and P4. The four-position rule encodes these as fixed predictions of one for P5, zero for P1, and zero outside the acidic class at P6 and the aliphatic class at P4. The other rows receive a fitted prediction. The numeric anchors and class membership are exploratory modeling choices. The article's preferences are qualitative summaries from the same study's reporter profiles. Both arms receive that published summary, including for the held position. Earlier structural work supports P5 tolerance, and independent work on off-target cleavage shows that hard sequence rules can miss real activity. [Huber et al., Fig. 2 discussion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60622-7) · [Structural study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3005794/) · [Off-target cleavage study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11129917/)

The original C10 fits use every training row. The restricted fits use only training rows where the context rule would defer to a model at prediction time. Without pair-specific AUC, the candidate models pool training rates or group them by enzyme, substitute residue, substitute chemical class, or whether the substitution preserves chemical class. Each grouped fit can either use its group mean or shrink it toward the pooled mean by the equivalent of 20 observations. Unseen groups fall back to the pooled mean. Within each outer fold, leave one entire training position out at a time, score all validation rows, and select the model with the lowest mean validation RMSE. The same inner folds select all-row versus restricted fitting separately for linear and isotonic calibration. The fixed restricted mean is also reported because it outperformed the selected no-reporter model on these outer folds.

The original mean, linear and isotonic models scored 0.35959, 0.31440 and 0.29342. Adding the four rules while keeping the all-row fits reverses the apparent reporter advantage. Selecting the fitting rows inside the outer training set restores a gain of 0.01880 for linear and 0.01928 for isotonic over the stronger fixed restricted mean on this panel. The largest improvements came from P2 and P1′. Both nested reporter models beat the nested sequence-context baseline where predictions could differ and tie it on P5 and P1 by construction. Relative to the restricted mean, the isotonic model loses narrowly at P6. P5 accounts for the largest error in every shared-context model. The P6 rule leaves only six test pairs open to the fitted prediction.

The primary interpretation is that adding context only at prediction time can reverse the conclusion of a model comparison. An audit using these rules should compare restricted fitting in both arms. The benchmark evaluates access to the study's qualitative position summaries, which already reflect reporter measurements at the held positions, and pair-specific AUC adds information on top of that context. The benchmark cannot estimate transfer to positions unavailable even in prior reporter profiles or to independent enzymes, assays or studies. Seven overlapping folds and shared control denominators preclude treating pairs as independent replicates. The analysis is exploratory, has no predefined practical accuracy threshold and provides no biological validation.

## Evidence and reproduction

The ledger records no accepted equivalent. The public C10 reassessment used a same-motif mean in held-enzyme folds, where measurements for the motif were available in training. Earlier attempts at this task added P5 and P1 context. The interrupted third attempt added P6 and P4 and observed differing results for the two fit populations. This package completes that investigation by testing whether training positions can select the fitting population under the same outer-fold scoring, and independently verifies both sets of results. ProtRec's pinned code, slope tables, issue and release snapshots were inspected. A ResearchGate tag-cleavage thread pointed to substrate accessibility as a separate issue and provided no paired calibration benchmark. The separate supplementary PDF could not be retrieved. [C10 reassessment](https://github.com/danduma/longevidence-challenge/tree/main/research/reassessment-2026-11-26) · [ResearchGate discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/post/I_have_a_protein_tagged_with_MBP_maltose_binding_proteinwith_a_TEV_cleavage_site_between_MBP_and_prot_How_to_improve_the_cleavage_of_the_tag)

Run `python3 analysis.py` to regenerate results and `python3 verify.py` to independently check the saved results with Python 3.10 or later and the local inputs. The verifier checks cohort and depth, reconstructs signed targets, recomputes all fold and pooled scores including the inner selections, and checks the direction of the decisive comparisons.
