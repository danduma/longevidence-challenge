# Does the optimized uniform surround close the grading gap?

This is a comparator test on Vitrify’s synthetic rat-heart phantom with extrapolated specific absorption rate (SAR). Only the surround is uniform. Myocardium and chambers retain their separate published loadings. The previous coordinator analysis held duty fixed at the released graded design and found a 0.001485 MPa objective penalty for a mean-loading-matched uniform surround on the 1.1 mm mesh. Here a single uniform loading and the three RF duty knots can move together. The graded reference is the exact released margin design.

## Two rate policies

Vitrify optimizes with a 51.5 °C/min floor on the 1.4 mm design grid and judges validation levels against 50 °C/min. A second test applies 51.5 °C/min to every grid. The released graded design passes its original policy and misses the stricter policy at 1.1 mm by 0.227 °C/min. Both tests retain the release’s nominal 70 s whole-specimen terminal temperature window. For event-stopped mechanics, the solver appends the interpolated first point when the coldest heart cell reaches −35 °C. The warmest specimen cell must stay below 0 °C at the event. The integration uses the release’s duty timebase for both score paths. [Vitrify optimizer](https://github.com/Marc-Dvci/Vitrify/blob/1d498ee5c583e0112a762e60892d576ec2e27f57/experiments/optimize_protocol.py), [released grid check](https://github.com/Marc-Dvci/Vitrify/blob/1d498ee5c583e0112a762e60892d576ec2e27f57/results/grid_convergence_margin.json), [Joshi et al. on the heart cutoff](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8823202/).

The search uses local COBYLA in four variables, scoring the smooth stress maximum on 1.4 mm through 16 s with thermal constraints at 1.4 and 1.1 mm. It uses the source bounds of 0–10 mg Fe/mL and 0.2–1 duty. The reported designs are feasible iterates. A bounds-filtered search found a release-policy iterate within 0.00003 MPa of the retained iterate. [Search trace](bounded-released.log). The retained designs were then evaluated through the thermal event at all four released spacings, with the original 70 s thermal screen checked independently. The source smooth maximum uses the three principal stresses in each specimen cell with a 0.02 MPa smoothing scale. It is unnormalized, so comparisons are paired within each mesh. The nominal 0.24 s mechanics cadence is rounded to the nearest thermal step count. On the finest grid the cadence after 16 s is nominally 1.8 s, with the same sampling rule for all designs. A separate full-cadence replay of the released graded design at 0.75 mm agreed in peak tension and smooth objective within 10⁻¹² MPa.

The rate-limiting voxel is myocardium within 0.2 s of startup, near −150 °C. This explains the practical pressure on the first duty knot and places the all-time rate margin below the glass transition in this model. It is a numerical benchmark constraint. The prior rat-heart paper’s physical interpretation uses the warming interval above glass transition. [Rate-limiting voxels](limiting-voxels.json), [Joshi et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8823202/).

The tuned uniform surround slightly improves both mechanics metrics on all four meshes under the release policy. The largest objective difference is under 0.002 MPa. The finer time-step check on the two coarser meshes retained the objective direction. The strict uniform witness stays within 0.0013 MPa of the released graded objective on all four meshes. That comparison uses the exact released grade even where it fails the strict screen. The strict witness clears its binding rate by 0.04286 °C/min, so the finding is limited to the released meshes. [Results](results.json), [cadence check](sensitivity.log).

The fixed-duty result could suggest that grading is needed to achieve the small stress advantage in this model. The reoptimized uniform candidate removes that advantage relative to the released grade, which makes it the stronger baseline for further comparisons.

## Prior work and scope

Joshi and Rabin's 2023 study already connected early thermal gradients with tensile stress and discussed time-varying power. Its uniform whole-specimen idealization also changes the heart loading. The 2022 study already tested uniform surrounding concentrations for thermal performance. The 2026 artery work considered loading and boundary-condition scenarios without systematic optimization. CryoNet discussed selectively increasing heating in lagging regions in 2002, and Biostasis discussed uniform warming through glass transition in 2017. The pinned Vitrify release and its margin commit optimize a graded surround and compare it with a fixed published uniform schedule. Its visible issue and pull-request lists were empty at retrieval. The added evidence is an independently scored, jointly tuned uniform-surround comparator that preserves the distinctions between heart compartments. [2023 heart mechanics](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0290063), [2026 artery mechanics](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0350078), [CryoNet](https://www.cryonet.org/cgi-bin/dsp.cgi?msg=18925), [Biostasis](https://www.biostasis.com/new-warming-breakthrough-for-cryopreserved-organs/).

The result is conditional on the phantom, the extrapolated SAR law, the released bounds, and the sampled mechanics. The nonconvex search supplies feasible witnesses and the graded comparator is the released design. A certified minimum, an optimized graded design under the stricter policy, continuum mesh convergence, and biological or fracture validation remain open. The policy’s absolute fit to real SAR and real organ stresses is outside this comparison.
