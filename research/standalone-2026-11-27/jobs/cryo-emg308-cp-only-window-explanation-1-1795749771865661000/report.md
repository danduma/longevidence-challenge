# Published M22 heat capacity explains part of the bag timing near the glass transition

The published M22 heat-capacity curve improves fixed-power predictions for the released 2 L EMG308 trace over −140 to 0 °C. Mean absolute error (MAE) falls from 1.97 s for the best tested constant to 1.56 s; direct transfer of the 1 L profile reaches 0.87 s. Away from the glass transition, the proxy explains much less. With temperature-dependent density over its supported −80 to 0 °C span, the model predicts that the last 20 °C window takes 1.021 times as long as the first. The observed ratio is 1.504. Under fixed power, matching that ratio would require a 47.3% larger late-to-early integrated heat-capacity ratio than the proxy supplies.

## Formulation and temperature support

Gangwar et al. describe suspending EMG308 in M22 prepared with water in place of LM5. Phatak describes the reference calorimetry formulation as M22 in LM5, with warming at 10 °C/min for M22. S4 in the liter-scale supplement cites Phatak for seven mass-specific heat values covering −149 to 0 °C. The curve is a proxy for the bag formulation, and the transition between the −130 and −119 °C knots is resolved only by linear interpolation. Gangwar et al. already attribute part of the rapid cold warming to lower glassy heat capacity, and their earlier CryoLetters Table 2 contains the same values. The new calculation tests whether those values suffice to reproduce the released timing profile. ## Held-out 2 L comparison

Each window uses the first upward crossings interpolated between successive time samples. Convert kJ/(kg K) to J/(kg K), multiply by density, and integrate over each window. Under a lumped fixed **volumetric** net power, time equals integrated heat capacity divided by that power. One common scale per span matches the mean total transit time of the 0.5 and 1 L traces. This scale uses no 2 L observation. Pooled mean constants match the same training total. The other comparators use the strongest smaller-bag constant among mean and median variants, direct 1 L transfer and a 1 L least-absolute-deviation line fitted on each span. The existing ledger already reported empirical transfer and warm-vial comparisons. This audit adds the heat-capacity model and its residuals.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| −140 to 0 | Fixed | 1.560 | 1.973 | 0.867 | 0.934 |
| −100 to 0 | Fixed | 1.708 | 1.620 | 0.805 | 0.701 |
| −80 to 0 | Fixed | 1.759 | 1.714 | 0.844 | 0.465 |
| −80 to 0 | Temperature-dependent | 1.906 | 1.714 | 0.844 | 0.465 |

Across −140 to 0 °C the proxy predicts a last-to-first duration ratio of 1.823 versus 2.013 observed, but still overpredicts the −120 to −100 and −80 to −60 °C intervals by about 2.65 and 2.67 s. Across −80 to 0 °C, the 0.5 and 1 L ratios are 1.536 and 1.590. The 2 L trace takes 11.20 s from −80 to −60 °C and 16.84 s from −20 to 0 °C, versus predictions of 14.65 and 14.97 s with variable density. The ratio mismatch is independent of the scale fit. If the proxy capacity were exact and a lumped balance held, the inferred late-window effective net power would be about 68% of the early-window power. The alternative fixed-power interpretation requires the 1.473-fold relative capacity correction reported above. The data allow combinations of these explanations.

The constant density and linear interpolation are modeling choices. This retrospective comparison uses one released curve per volume, one-second sampling and dependent windows. Actual power deposition, heat exchange, formulation effects and thermometry can all contribute to the residual. The results establish insufficiency of the **published proxy** under the stated model on these traces. Resolving the actual bag mixture's contribution needs matched calorimetry and density or independent power and boundary measurements. A next public-data test can use Figure 7b to ask whether the post-transition mismatch varies across positions within the 2 L bag, with probes treated as dependent.
