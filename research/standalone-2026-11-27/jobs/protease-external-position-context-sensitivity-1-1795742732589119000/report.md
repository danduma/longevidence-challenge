# The reporter advantage survives independently sourced context in the ProtRec panel

With equal training weights, the linear and isotonic reporter fits achieved mean held-position RMSEs of 0.29354 and 0.28897. The sequence-context baseline scored 0.33742. The earlier audit found the opposite ordering under four hard rules derived from ProtRec's own reporter profiles. Here, reweighting training positions lowered error in all three model families, but the reporter advantage was already present. This is a sensitivity result for an existing affinity-tag reagent benchmark.

## What changed

The benchmark uses the same 780 matched enzyme–substrate pairs, including 170 signed negative rate estimates. Each outer fold holds out one of seven substitution positions across all six enzymes. Canonical measurements define the relative initial-rate target and canonical pairs are excluded from fitting and scoring. The reporter methods require corrected AUC for each held pair. The earlier analysis applied fixed predictions of zero or one on selected rows and compared all-row fitting with fitting on rows where the rules deferred to the model. Its hard-rule comparison scored 0.19983 for the context mean and 0.21229 and 0.21494 for the all-row reporter fits. [ProtRec paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60622-7) · [C10 reassessment](https://github.com/danduma/longevidence-challenge/tree/main/research/reassessment-2026-11-26)

The independent context uses four position-wide priors. The 2010 structural comparison describes solvent exposure and substitution tolerance at P5, extensive TEV contacts at P6, a substrate pocket at P4 where even a conservative substitution can reduce efficiency, and canonical substrate interactions at P1. The 2024 tag-cleavage study documents recombinant-protein exceptions and supports leaving room for departures from the preferences. We encoded these qualitative observations as illustrative anchor values of 0.8, 0.2, 0.2 and 0.1 at P5, P6, P4 and P1 respectively. These values are analyst choices. They were not taken as kinetic estimates from either article. The rules use no measurements from the held ProtRec position. The ResearchGate discussion about tag accessibility concerns a fusion protein and provides no calibration evidence. [Structural comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3005794/) · [Recombinant protein study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11129917/) · [Specialist discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/post/I_have_a_protein_tagged_with_MBP_maltose_binding_proteinwith_a_TEV_cleavage_site_between_MBP_and_prot_How_to_improve_the_cleavage_of_the_tag)

For each model family, inner leave-position-out validation minimizes mean position RMSE. It selects the fraction of the prediction drawn from each applicable anchor from 0, 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75. It also selects whether rows from positions with anchors receive full, half or quarter weight in fitting. The equal-weight regime fixes the training weight at one everywhere and retains inner tuning of the anchor fraction. The no-reporter baseline selects among pooled, enzyme, substitute-residue, substitute-class and class-preservation means, with grouped means optionally shrunk toward the weighted pooled mean by 20 full-weight observations. The reporter arms fit a line or an increasing isotonic curve with clipped endpoints and linear interpolation. All models use the same inner and outer test rows. An exploratory sensitivity comparison fixes the fraction at 0.75 in all arms and retains inner selection of the training weights and baseline type.

## What the comparisons establish

| Regime | Sequence-context baseline | Linear reporter | Isotonic reporter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Equal training weights, tuned anchor fraction | 0.33742 | 0.29354 | 0.28897 |
| Tuned training weights and anchor fraction | 0.31298 | 0.26544 | 0.26354 |
| Tuned training weights, fixed 0.75 anchor fraction | 0.24675 | 0.22113 | 0.22313 |

These are equally weighted means of the seven outer position RMSEs. The fit-population reversal seen with the original hard context did not recur. Inner validation selected quarter-weight fitting at six positions and equal weights at P5 in each family when both weights and anchor fraction were tunable. Both tuned reporter models lose to the tuned context baseline at P3. At P5, tuning drops the anchor entirely in all families and gives the largest error in every regime. Fixing the anchor fraction improves P5 errors in all arms. The fixed context baseline beats both jointly tuned reporters across the seven positions. Under the matched fixed-fraction regime, both reporter arms again have lower error than that baseline. The fixed-fraction sensitivity changes only P5 predictions relative to the jointly tuned regime.

The value of the reporter depends on the matched context comparator. The original conclusion that restricted fitting is needed to recover a gain should be scoped to the original hard rules. The independent priors and softer weighting alter several assumptions together, so this experiment does not identify which individual change removed the reversal. The grades are illustrative and the fixed-fraction comparison is retrospective. Seven overlapping training sets, six related assay reagents and shared canonical denominators limit inference. The experiment supplies neither an independent panel evaluation nor a practical error threshold. AUC correction was taken from the frozen matched table.

## Reproduction and attribution

Run `python3 analysis.py` to regenerate results and `python3 verify.py` to check them independently with Python 3.10 or later. Verification reconstructs 840 deposited relative rates from signed slopes, checks the matched keys and depth criteria, independently selects each model with inner validation, and asserts each new fold and aggregate score. The separate verifier uses the lower convex hull for isotonic fitting. The scripts reuse code from the prior standalone comparison.
